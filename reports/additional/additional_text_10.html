A free energy per protein

19 November 2026

1. The theory

A pull-down is a partition. Each protein i, in each condition c, is divided between a bead-bound pool and a free pool, and both pools are measured: the IP gives the bound side, the post-IP supernatant gives the free side. Their log ratio is a binding constant,

log2Ki,c=parti,c+Cc,part=ip−sup,\log_2 K_{i,c} = \mathrm{part}_{i,c} + C_c , \qquad \mathrm{part} = \mathrm{ip} - \mathrm{sup},

where CcC_c is a single per-condition constant absorbing the free bait concentration, the bead capacity, and the fact that the IP and supernatant runs were median-normalised separately. The free energy follows:

ΔGi,c=−RTln2(parti,c+Cc).\Delta G_{i,c} = -RT\ln 2 \;\bigl(\mathrm{part}_{i,c} + C_c\bigr).

CcC_c is not measured, so absolute ΔG\Delta G is unobtainable. In a contrast between two conditions it collapses to one number shared by every protein,

ΔΔGi(c→c′)=−RTln2[(parti,c′−parti,c)+(Cc′−Cc)],\Delta\Delta G_i (c \to c') = -RT\ln 2\;\bigl[(\mathrm{part}_{i,c'} - \mathrm{part}_{i,c}) + (C_{c'}-C_c)\bigr],

so a per-protein ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G is determined up to a single additive constant per contrast, and differences between proteins are fully determined. The conversion is RTln2RT\ln 2 per log2 unit: 0.4245 kcal/mol at 35 °C, 0.4272 at 37 °C, 0.4355 at 43 °C. For temperature, van ’t Hoff (dlnK/d(1/T)=−ΔH/R\mathrm{d}\ln K/\mathrm{d}(1/T) = -\Delta H/R) makes one log2 unit of change across 35 → 43 °C equal to an apparent ΔH\Delta H of 16.8 kcal/mol.

Three conditions have to hold for this to be physics rather than bookkeeping: the perturbation must act during binding rather than only during the wash; the bait must not deplete the client, so that free ≈ total; and the dose-response must take the form the underlying physics dictates. The first is now settled for the salt series — the lab confirms salt is present in the incubation.

A further step would read stability: if the chaperone binds exposed or unfolded state, then bound ∝fu\propto f_u, and while the native state dominates, log2fu≈−ΔGunf/RTln2\log_2 f_u \approx -\Delta G_{unf}/RT\ln 2, so enrichment would be a linear readout of stability. Section 3 rejects this step.

2. What can be proven

The partition is a property of the protein, not of the experiment. GA_22 and GA_24 are independent experiments — separate lysates, separate MS runs, different cofactor arms — sharing only the bait and the salt points. Their per-protein ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G at 120 mM KCl agree at r = 0.894 [0.884, 0.902], n = 6156, against a maximum attainable c1c2=0.943\sqrt{c_1c_2} = 0.943 set by the two reliabilities. That is 94.7 % of the attainable correlation (Figure 2). Whatever the quantity is, it is reproducible and protein-specific.

The precision is sufficient to rank proteins.

The dose-response has the form the theory requires. Regressing the larger salt step on the smaller one per protein, with an errors-in-variables (Deming) slope so that noise in the smaller step does not attenuate it:

Run steps measured slope linear in [salt] log-linear in ionic strength
GA_22 part 60 → 120 mM 1.544 [1.502, 1.590] 2.00 1.55
GA_24 part 60 → 120 mM 1.589 [1.551, 1.635] 2.00 1.55
GA_20 ip 75 → 150 mM 1.472 [1.448, 1.499] 2.00 1.51

Linear dependence on concentration is excluded in all three. The log-linear prediction is the Record–Lohman form for release of electrostatic ion pairs, and GA_20 — at different concentrations from the other two — lands on its own prediction (Figure 1). One caveat: the prediction assumes a 50 mM buffer contribution to ionic strength; across a plausible 25–100 mM range it moves between 1.44 and 1.68, so these data fix the functional form but not the buffer floor.

The bait does not deplete the client. If salt merely stripped protein off the beads, that material would reappear in the supernatant and the two contrasts would be negatively correlated. The measured slope of the supernatant contrast on the IP contrast is +0.15 (GA_22) and +0.07 (GA_24) — not negative. Free ≈ total, which is the regime the derivation assumes.

The temperature response contains a real and specific thermodynamic component. Partial correlation of the 43 vs 35 °C response with meltome TmT_m, controlling lysate abundance and length, cluster-bootstrapped over the 30 % identity groups: −0.159 [−0.181, −0.136] for DNAJA1 and −0.192 [−0.215, −0.170] for DNAJB11. It survives net charge in a joint model (βTm\beta_{T_m} = −0.185 and −0.235, both intervals excluding zero). Critically it is specific: TmT_m does not predict the salt response (+0.061 in GA_22, −0.007 in GA_24 — inconsistent in sign between two runs of the same contrast), which is what a genuine stability term should look like and what a generic covariate should not (Figure 4).

3. What can be discounted

The temperature response is not two-state unfolding. Three independent arguments, all framed on spreads rather than levels, because per-sample median normalisation forces the median response to zero and makes absolute response levels uninterpretable.

Scale. Below the transition the predicted response does not depend on TmT_m at all — at ΔH\Delta H = 100 kcal/mol it is 5.52, 5.87, 5.94 and 5.96 log2 units for TmT_m = 45, 48.5, 52 and 57 °C. The model therefore predicts almost no protein-to-protein variation. Observed 5–95 % spread is 3.44 log2; the largest the model can produce, maximised over every enthalpy from 15 to 300 kcal/mol, is 0.47 log2 at ΔH\Delta H = 46 — a 7.4-fold shortfall, and no choice of enthalpy closes it (Figure 5).

Correlation. The explicit two-state predictor — log2 of the unfolded-fraction ratio between 43 and 35 °C, computed from each protein’s own TmT_m — correlates with the data at r = −0.005 to +0.044, which is worse than a plain linear TmT_m term.

Shape. The observed response is an inverted U in TmT_m, rising to a peak in the high 40s and falling away on both sides. Two-state unfolding predicts a monotone saturating curve (Figure 3).

The stability signal established in Section 2 is therefore real but minor — it accounts for roughly 3 % of the variance in the temperature response — and the bulk of that response is something else.

The current temperature range cannot measure TmT_m, even with perfect data. This follows from the same flatness. Between 35 and 43 °C every protein melting above about 45 °C is native-dominant, and in that regime the response reports ΔH\Delta H and is independent of TmT_m. TmT_m only becomes observable once the transition is crossed. This is a property of the experimental design, not of measurement quality, and no amount of replication or MS time will recover it.

Net charge is not evidence of ion-pair release. Charge does predict the salt response, consistently in sign across both runs (+0.127 in GA_22, +0.174 in GA_24). But it predicts the temperature response at least as strongly (−0.241 and −0.199), where ion-pair release is irrelevant. It fails the specificity test that TmT_m passed, and is best read as a generic compositional covariate. The electrostatic argument rests on the log-linear dose-response alone, which does not depend on charge.

4. What these data cannot decide

Absolute ΔG\Delta G. CcC_c was removed deliberately by median normalisation. Every number here is relative to the median protein. A spike-in standard, or a reference set assumed invariant, would restore it.

Whether the binding is to the bait or to the resin. No experiment includes a bait-free IP. A charge-dependent, salt-reversible interaction is exactly as consistent with a charged resin as with HSPB1, and nothing in this dataset separates them.

Whether the bait is limiting. In the joint model, lysate abundance carries the largest coefficient (+0.344 and +0.374) — larger than TmT_m or charge — in a within-protein contrast where abundance ought to have cancelled. That is either a dynamic-range artefact or real competition for a limited bait, and the difference matters: under competition every ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G is compressed by an unknown factor.

Whether the wash is at the incubation salt. If the washes run at a common salt instead, the measured ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G carries an off-rate term of the same sign as the equilibrium term, making it an upper bound rather than an estimate.

Whether a subunit’s ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G is its own. Residuals are shared within Complex Portal complexes, so for a subunit the quantity may belong to the assembly.

5. What follows

The salt ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G is usable now, as a per-protein quantity, on the strength of Section 2 — it reproduces across independent experiments at 95 % of the attainable limit, it has the precision to rank proteins, and its dose-response has the right functional form. It should be reported as a ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G relative to the median protein, never as an absolute binding energy.

The temperature ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G should be reported as an apparent ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G and not as a stability. The stability component is real and specific but small, and the dominant part of the signal has neither the magnitude nor the shape of unfolding.

Four experiments resolve the four open items, in priority order:

Experiment Resolves
Bait-free IP at every salt point bait vs resin — the one fraction no experiment has
Bait titration at 0.25 / 1 / 4× whether the bait is limiting, and so whether ΔΔG\Delta\Delta G is compressed
Temperature 40–60 °C in 4 °C steps crosses the transition; the only route to a per-protein TmT_m
A spike-in standard restores CcC_c, and with it absolute ΔG\Delta G

The temperature recommendation is worth separating from the earlier argument that the 2 °C step was simply too small. That argument was about precision. The argument here is about identifiability: within 35–43 °C the response is mathematically insensitive to TmT_m, so no per-protein stability exists to be measured in this window at any precision.
