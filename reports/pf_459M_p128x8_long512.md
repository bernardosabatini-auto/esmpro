## Outcome
Two findings. (1) **Length generalisation was already there.** The 256-window model, run at 512 with an extended position table and never having seen a protein longer than 256 residues, folds 257-512-residue proteins at 0.560 (AFDB val) and 0.730 (long CASP domains). The relative-position attention bias and the per-residue latent carry over; absolute positions barely matter. (2) **The long data adds a little, not a lot, at fine-tune dose:** +0.012 on the 1,000 long validation proteins (0.560 -> 0.572, 59 -> 63 % correct folds, best-of-4 0.593 -> 0.612), +0.006 on the 29 long CASP domains, and nothing on the full CASP set. The long-val curve was flat at 0.583-0.587 (selection subset) from epoch 5, so more epochs at this learning rate would not have changed it. On short proteins the run is the **best model to date on every set** (held-out 0.770 / 89 %, novel-fold subsets 0.582 and 0.524, CASP 0.710), a further +0.003-0.008 over the control fine-tune, consistent with the second-cosine-cycle effect plus a small benefit from the extra data. Coverage 100 % at all 12 evaluations and all scoring.

## What it changed
`last_pf_459M_p128x8_long512.ckpt` is the current best set of weights for both length ranges (long val 0.572, long CASP 0.736, held-out 0.770). The gap to ESMFold2-Fast on short CASP is now 0.03. The long-protein ceiling of this recipe is clearly not the data window: an AFDB long-val TM of 0.57 against 0.77 on short held-out says the model's *capacity* per residue pair (pair 128 x 8 with checkpointing, 25 sampling steps) is the limit at 300-500 residues. The from-scratch 512 run now on the H200s (`pf_459M_p128x8_long512_scratch`: R = 4, late-t resampling, all data, 40 epochs) tests whether training at the long window from the start changes that; if it also plateaus near 0.6 on long val, the next lever is the pair track's depth at long L or a larger trunk, not more long data.
