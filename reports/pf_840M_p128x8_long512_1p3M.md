## Reading
1. **Doubling the trunk buys 0.005 to 0.008 on the short and held-out sets, and nothing on long proteins.** It is the best model we have on five of seven rows, by a margin that is real but small, and it ties or loses on the two long-protein rows.
2. **It did so with fewer passes over the data, not more.** 12 epochs of 1.26M proteins is 15.1M protein-passes against the 459M run's 19.4M (30 epochs of 646k); at R = 8 against R = 4 it saw 121M flow samples against 77.5M. Parameters and unique proteins went up, protein-passes went down.
3. **The number is an underestimate, because the schedule never annealed.** CLAUDE.md bug 5 applied here: `steps_per_epoch` was computed as 13,125 from the nominal batch size against 8,600 measured, so 12 epochs covered 65 % of the cosine and the run ended at lr 7.94e-5, a quarter of peak. The flat, noisy tail (0.609, 0.600, 0.604, 0.603) is what an unannealed cosine looks like. A 2-epoch anneal from the epoch-9 weights with the measured step count is running (`pf_840M_p128x8_long512_anneal`, job 48973671) and its scores will be added here.
4. **The gap to ESMFold2-Fast is unchanged at 0.02 on CASP and 0.03 on long proteins**, at 0.32-0.48 s per protein against its 0.84-3.9 s. Scale has not closed it; it has moved us 0.005.
5. Caveat: the scored checkpoint is the last (epoch 12), not the selected best (epoch 9), because the scorers were gated on job completion. The anneal supersedes both.

## What it changed
The main line is now 840M on 1.26M proteins and is the best model on the short and held-out sets. But the return on doubling the trunk is 0.005-0.008, the same order as the pair track's contribution and smaller than the 0.02 gap to ESMFold2-Fast. Combined with `pair_free_ablation.md`, the evidence is that neither more parameters nor the triangular pair track is where the remaining accuracy is.
